The Idol & The Rituals

From the painting of the eyes before Mahalaya dawn to the immersion at the ghat on Bisarjan evening.

The idol

An Ekchala idol, daker saaj

The Goddess and her children are cast together on a single arched frame — the traditional Ekchala style this house has never departed from.

Since 1905, the idol has worn daker saaj: ornaments of silver-foil and shola-pith once imported by "daak" (post) from Krishnanagar, a term that gives the style its name.

The idol's face is modelled each year by the same sculptor's family from Kumartuli, following a wooden template kept in the house since the 1920s.

Sculptor & craftsmen

The hands behind the idol

Building begins on Rath Yatra, with clay from the Hooghly's own bank mixed by the sculptor's workshop in Kumartuli.

Unique to this house

Traditions found nowhere else

Unlike most Durga Pujas in Kolkata, this Puja follows a few customs found only in this family.

Hand-calculated Sandhi timing

The family's almanac-keeper calculates the exact 24-minute junction of Sandhi Puja by hand, cross-checking against the printed panjika.

Unwashed courtyard floor

After Sindoor Khela, the courtyard's red floor is left unwashed until the following morning.

The eldest daughter-in-law's rite

The Kola Bou snan on Sashthi morning is performed only by the household's eldest daughter-in-law.
