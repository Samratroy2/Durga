About the Puja

A tradition carried by many hands

The Roy Bari Durga Puja is a living tradition. Its continuity comes not only from rituals, but also from the craftsmen, priests, family members, photographs, memories, newspaper records, and stories passed from one generation to the next.

01 · The Idol Makers

The Hands Behind the Goddess

The making of the Durga idol is itself part of the Puja tradition. From the first framework to the final decoration, each stage depends on the skill and experience of the craftsmen.

A Craft Passed Down

Generations of craftsmanship

The idol associated with the Roy Bari Puja has been shaped through a continuing relationship between the family and its craftsmen.

Bamboo, straw, clay, sculpting, painting and decoration each require a different stage of careful work.

The finished Goddess carries the visual character of the Roy Bari tradition while also reflecting the individual skill of the present maker.

02 · The Priests & Purohits

The Voices Behind the Sacred Rituals

The priests and purohits play an important role in maintaining the spiritual traditions of the Roy Bari Durga Puja. Through mantras, prayers, offerings and prescribed rituals, they guide the ceremonial observances throughout the Puja.

Sacred Service

A tradition of worship

The priests associated with the Puja carry the responsibility of conducting the traditional ceremonies according to the customs followed by the family.

From the invocation of the Goddess to the daily offerings and concluding ceremonies, their presence forms an essential part of the Puja.

Their knowledge of Sanskrit mantras, rituals and traditional practices helps preserve the ceremonial character of the Roy Bari Durga Puja.
